Weight-Space Programming
An Independent Field Guide

Can a general-purpose LLM read, repair, and even write a neural network's weights - by reasoning alone? Eight experiments, two mechanisms, and one honest scoreboard.

Read · Fix · Write - the three claims of the source post (2 Nov 2026), re-examined.

IF YOU READ ONE PAGE The finding. A general-purpose LLM read small networks' weights and named their behaviour, fixed a defect with one reasoned edit, and wrote working networks from scratch - without gradient descent. The headline number. Editing 0.016% of one model's weights raised MATH-500 from 7.9% to 48.1% and cut runaway "thinking" from 93% to 33% of answers - and a negative control on a different model moved nothing. The honest caveats. Single runs throughout; baselines missing; tasks author-designed. "Sparks" is exactly the right word.

WSP-Bench-Lite ■ SPECULATION (proposal)

Community benchmark proposal (clearly Het's, not the authors'): three tracks Read / Fix / Write; tasks authored by non-competing teams; ≥5 seeds; mandatory baselines (decoding tweaks, standard direction ablation, human interpreter, same heuristic as code); metrics: success, edit sparsity s = ‖Δ‖₀/P, functional drift D = E KL(p_θ‖p_θ'), budget-curve fidelity, cost; pre-registered prompts; public logs. Traces are logged but never counted as evidence.
